- **Abstract:** Subtherapeutic rifampicin exposure drives treatment failure and acquired resistance in tuberculosis. We aimed to develop and externally validate a concentration-driven machine learning (ML) model predicting rifampicin systemic exposure (AUC0-24h) from sparse clinical sampling, addressing the structural biases and limited generalizability of conventional Bayesian precision dosing. Twenty-six published population pharmacokinetic models were benchmarked against real-world TDM data from a French cohort; the five externally compatible models were used to simulate a training cohort of 4,500 virtual patients. An XGBoost regression model was trained to predict AUC0-24h from a two-point sampling strategy and evaluated against Maximum A Posteriori Bayesian Estimation (MAP-BE) and dynamic Model Averaging (MA). External validation was performed on two completely independent datasets: a US cohort of healthy volunteers and a Brazilian cohort of inpatient tuberculosis cases, including HIV-coinfected patients. A sampling strategy at 2 and 8 hours post-dose provided optimal accuracy. Across the combined cross-continental validation cohorts (n = 40), the concentration-driven ML model showed consistent external predictive performance.
