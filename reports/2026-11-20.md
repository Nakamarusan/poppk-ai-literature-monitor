- **Abstract:** Background: Therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) enables individualized pharmacotherapy for drugs with high interpatient variability and concentration-dependent effects. Conventional Bayesian approaches, although clinically established, may not fully capture pharmacokinetic complexity in dynamic or heterogeneous patient populations. Artificial intelligence (AI) and machine-learning methods offer complementary capabilities, including modeling nonlinear relationships and integrating high-dimensional clinical data. Methods: Peer-reviewed studies that applied AI or machine-learning methods to TDM between January 2017 and April 2026 were reviewed in accordance with Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses 2020 guidelines. Studies involving adult and pediatric populations were included, whereas reviews, editorials, and animal studies were excluded. Results: Forty-seven studies covering a range of drugs, including vancomycin, tacrolimus, antiepileptics, mycophenolic acid, methotrexate, and voriconazole, were included. Tree-based ensemble methods, particularly eXtreme Gradient Boosting, random forest, and gradient boosting, were the most frequently applied algorithms.
